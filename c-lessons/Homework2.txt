2. Read the article Does OO Sync with How We Think? by Les Hatton in the May/June 1998 issue of
IEEE Software, (this article is available on IEEE xplore). Briefly answer each of the following questions.
(a) The author concludes that OO paradigm that at first sight is similar to the people logical thought process in reality
is a something different. To prove this he used the comparison with the human short-term and long-term memories and we 
observe that the methods provided by OO does not fit into this logical process and for a long term period proven to be inefficient 
way to actually implement things. Thus, his opinion states that it is not possible for OO to be the silver bullet that will result 
in quantum leap for software engineering.
(b) As I understood the main costs related to software generally come from the corrective maintenance stage when the software 
problems are fixed. This is mostly due to the reason that it is one of the main part of software life-cycle so that it
takes twice as much time to fix the problems on this stage than on the stage of development.
(c) In this article, the main experiment was that corrective maintenance cost was tracked for the two types of
programming languages. First is the OO type, and the other is conventional type. The results that were observed 
show that the cost is much higher for OO type as there were much more time spent fixing the problems and on agraphical
data we saw that this indeed was the case here. There were noticeable shift of the OO graph to the side where more time
was needed in order to fix everything.
